## 2. Index

`content/swipe/index.csv` — 878 rows, committed separately as "Add swipe index".

Per row: path, folder, type, what it shows, colour words, legible brand,
legible composition, how it is styled, note, **five dominant hexes with
shares**, nearest Soft Autumn anchor and its ΔE2000, palette confidence and the
reason for it, skin share, subject share, shortlist flag, a suggested
engine-convention `slot_name`, and whether the image was seen on a contact
sheet or opened.

**How the hexes were made, and where not to trust them.** Colour extraction is
the engine's own `matcher.extract_colours` (`matching.md` §1) — imported, not
reimplemented — run at k=5 over masked pixels. The mask removes iOS and Safari
chrome geometrically, then up to five near-neutral modal buckets at the extreme
ends of lightness (page background, white text column, dark UI band).

Two honest limits:

- **Skin.** The skin band overlaps two real anchors: dusty rose sits at hue
  34.7° and salmon at 42.5°, inside any band drawn around skin. This is CLAUDE.md
  hard rule 6 in another guise — a colour test cannot separate skin from a rose
  garment. So the mask is applied, `skin_share` is recorded per row, and any row
  above 15% is marked **low** confidence with the reason stated rather than
  silently resolved.
- **Studio backdrops.** On a product-page screenshot the garment is often a
  minority of the frame and the studio backdrop survives masking. `hex1` on
  those rows is frequently the backdrop, not the piece. Every such row is capped
  at **medium** or **low** with the reason `top cluster NN% — may still be a
  studio backdrop`. On those rows the `what_it_shows` / `styled_how` /
  `composition` columns — written from opening the image — are the reliable part,
  and the hexes are a starting point, not a measurement.

Confidence across the 878: **high 174, medium 400, low 304**. That distribution
is the honest one: after the backdrop fix, most product-page rows correctly
demoted themselves. Before the fix the same run reported 436 high, which was
wrong — it was scoring "I removed a lot of background" as "I found the garment". The anchor-distribution skew toward `warm charcoal`, `stone`
and `cream` in the index is partly this artefact and should not be read as a
finding about the library's palette.

## 5. Styling analysis — seventeen worked examples

*The brief asked for ten to twelve. The library supported more and I have kept
them all rather than choose: seven observed, seven built, three image-to-outfit.
Ten of the seventeen fail a rule, which is the useful half.*

Every filename below was opened. Hexes are from `index.csv` where the row is
**high** confidence, and re-sampled from the garment region of the opened image
where the row is **medium** or **low** — which, on product pages, is most of
them. Where a hex below differs from that row's `hex1`, `hex1` is the studio
backdrop and the value quoted here is the garment. Where a verdict is
quoted it is the engine's, run by importing `engine.palette`, `engine.generators`
and `engine.matcher` and scoring against `soft_autumn` with the `teal_ochre`
direction applied — 26 generated pairs. Nothing in `engine/` or `frameworks/`
was modified.

### a) Observed — how a piece is styled in the image

**O1 · `products/IMG_0223.png` — Aspesi camel corduroy jacket**
*(GIACCA IN VELLUTO A COSTE, colour BEIGE; the Italian details text names
garment-dyed 500-line cotton corduroy)*

Proportion: a short, straight, single-breasted jacket over a full mid-calf
skirt, so the break sits at the high hip and the skirt does all the volume.
Layering: jacket open over a fine stone knit, nothing tucked. Texture mix: heavy
corduroy pile / flat fine knit / matte skirt cloth / smooth black boot — four
surfaces, no two alike. Colour pairing: camel `A87C4A` over dark olive, with
stone between them. The unexpected move is the **black boot on a soft season** —
and it is legal, because `seasons.yaml` gives Soft Autumn
`black: below_waist_or_hardware`. Why it works in the frameworks' terms: camel
and dark olive are a **Muted** pair, and the engine names it — `Dark Olive and
Camel`, hue gap in the 40–100° band, both under the 0.45 relative-chroma cap,
value gap carrying it.

**O2 · `products/IMG_0283.png` — Lardini SHORT BEIGE PEACOAT**

Proportion: a cropped camel peacoat over very wide, very long chocolate
trousers that break over the shoe. Two garments, two colours, nothing else.
Texture mix: melton wool against a flat suiting cloth — the weakest texture mix
of the twelve, and the outfit survives it because the **value gap is enormous**
(camel around L\* 59, chocolate around L\* 19). This is `combinations.md`'s
"the clarity comes from the value" in one picture. The unexpected move is that
there is no third colour at all; the black shirt underneath is invisible.

**O3 · `fashion shows ss27/IMG_0843.png` — rust knit and rust wrap skirt**
*(vogue.com runway gallery; tab reads `Kallmeye…`, not resolved)*

Head-to-toe one rust, cardigan and skirt, with an oxblood tote and a taupe pump.
Proportion: knit-on-knit, the cardigan hem cutting the skirt at the high hip; a
front slit gives the column a moving edge. The unexpected move is the taupe shoe
— a lighter neutral under a saturated column, which stops the look ending in a
dark full stop. **This one fails the rule and works anyway**: knit `943D2A`
against tote `5A2A22` is a hue gap of 4.0° — the Tonal band — with ΔL\* of only
**14.0** against a required 25. The engine returns `NO GENERATOR`. What carries
it is surface: rib knit, wrap crepe, polished leather. `matching.md` has a
texture check but it only fires to flag *flat*; it cannot credit texture for
rescuing a failed value gap. Worth saying out loud in a post.

**O4 · `fashion shows ss27/IMG_0860.png` — ochre pile knit, plum skirt, teal set**
*(vogue.com; tab reads `Tory Bur…`, not resolved)*

An ochre beaded-fringe sweater over a deep plum crackled midi skirt, plum
beanie, chartreuse-gold sandals, against a strong teal wall on a terracotta
floor. The unexpected move is that **the room is half the outfit**. Knit
`9C6A22` against backdrop `1D4559` is hue gap **172.7°**, Δ relative chroma
**0.03**, ΔL\* **21.5** — the engine returns **opposition**. That is Teal and
Ochre, the reference pair, and the garment only supplies one half of it. Which is
the honest, slightly deflating thing to tell a reader: on a runway the set
designer is doing work your bedroom wall is not.

**O5 · `products/IMG_0236.png` — Barena Venezia check jacket**

A brown-and-rust check wool jacket over navy pinstripe wide trousers, a navy
knit showing at the waist. Two patterns at once, at different scales. It works
because both patterns are *quiet* — the check reads as a mid-brown at two paces
and the pinstripe reads as navy — so the eye receives two textures and two
colours, not four. Texture mix is the whole argument. Proportion: boxy cropped
jacket, wide trouser, the volume stacked rather than tapered.

**O6 · `fashion shows ss27/IMG_0863.png` — black dress, butter-yellow sash**
*(vogue.com; tab reads `Proenza`)*

A black draped V-neck dress with a pale butter-yellow sash at the waist, a deep
burgundy draped panel, oxblood shoes. The unexpected move is that **the only
light thing in the outfit is at the waist**, which is where a value break moves
the eye. One accent, placed, not scattered. Frameworks reading: black at the
face is out for Soft Autumn (`below_waist_or_hardware`) — so this is a look to
learn the *placement* from, not the colours.

**O7 · `products/IMG_0252.png` — Massimo Alba CARLOTTA2 corduroy jacket**
*(cotton corduroy, colour Taupe)*

Flat shot above, model shot below: taupe corduroy double-breasted jacket worn
with an **ochre knitted headscarf**. That headscarf is the whole lesson. Under
`matching.md` §2 a scarf is an accessory with `near_face: true`, which means it
takes the top slot's rules and becomes the face colour — so a quiet taupe jacket
plus one ochre accessory puts ochre next to the face and the jacket stops being
a face garment. One small piece changes which rule the big piece is judged by.

## 7. Things I think are wrong

Reported, not fixed (CLAUDE.md hard rule 8; and the brief puts `engine/` and
`frameworks/` out of bounds).

**7.1 · The neutral boundary is knife-edge for exactly the colours this wardrobe
is made of — and extraction noise crosses it.**

`combinations.md` §5 defines a neutral as relative chroma ≤ 0.15. Neutrals sit
out of the generators and pair with anything in palette. Chromatics must match a
generated pair.

The same oatmeal knit, sampled from two screenshots of it, comes out at
`C6BBA9` (relative chroma **0.13** → neutral) and `C3B49B` (relative chroma
**0.19** → chromatic). In outfit B2 it is a neutral and the outfit is **valid**.
In outfit B5 it is a chromatic, pairs with nothing, and the outfit **fails**.
A 0.06 difference in a measured value flips a styling verdict.

This is not a sampling problem I can fix by sampling better; warm neutrals —
stone, oatmeal, warm charcoal, warm mid grey — genuinely live within a few
hundredths of 0.15, and they are the tier `seasons.yaml` says should be 55% of
the wardrobe. The consequence is B5: **a brand's own styling, which reads
perfectly, is rejected on the pairing of its two quietest pieces.**

Worth considering (your call, not mine): a band rather than a line — treat
0.12–0.20 as *near-neutral* and let those pair as ground while reporting the
ambiguity — or a Tonal exemption for two in-palette colours under, say, 0.25
relative chroma with any value gap. I have not touched the file.

**7.2 · The contrast check fires on almost everything.**

Seven of the nine built outfits were flagged *higher contrast than your natural
colouring*. Soft Autumn's target is `low`, and any outfit containing a dark shoe
and a light knit exceeds it. If it fires on nearly every real outfit it stops
carrying information. The threshold may need to be relative to the outfit's
*visible* items, or to exclude shoes, which cover little and sit furthest from
the face.

**7.3 · `staples/README.md` says "the five slots in `matching.md`". There are eight.**

`matching.md` §3 lists top, bottom, dress, layer, base, shoes, bag, accessory.
`staples/` has files for five. So **there is no staples slot for a layer** — and a
coat or jacket is the single most common autumn gap, and the most common slot in
your own library (191 of the suggested names). Source 2 structurally cannot fill
it. Also note `scope.md` correctly records that `staples/` has no sampled hex
values yet, so source 2 cannot fill anything at all today.

**7.4 · Two editorial rules in the brief are not written down in this repository,
and one of them contradicts a framework.**

You asked me to say so rather than add them.

- *"Price is never a criterion or a selling point."* **Not in the repo**, and
  `horizons.md` §3d ranks the realistic next move by
  `outfits_unlocked × palette_improvement ÷ price_band` — price is an explicit
  ranking input. `material-rubric.md` field 4 also scores price coherence.
  These are not the same claim as "price is never a criterion", and one of them
  has to give.
- *"Runway and luxury houses are colour and trend inspiration only, never
  recommended products."* **Not in the repo anywhere** — zero matches. And it
  sits awkwardly with `matching.md` §5 source 3, which searches `brands/` for
  recommendations, where `brands/` contains Max Mara, Akris, Brunello Cucinelli
  and Zegna, all `price_band: luxury`.
- *"Sustainable preferred, not required"* **is** written down — CLAUDE.md's
  positioning paragraph and hard rule 4. That one is fine.

I have followed all three as instructions for this session and added none of
them to any file.

**7.5 · Nothing in `brands/` can currently pass or fail.**

All fifteen `rubric_score` fields are blank and `material-rubric.md`'s
thresholds are unfilled (`Total required to pass: ___ / 15`). CLAUDE.md hard
rule 3 says the rubric is the source of truth for whether a brand passes. Right
now it cannot answer, which means no product recommendation can be justified
under the repository's own rules.

**7.6 · The backlog's one time-limited entry expires in 16 days with nothing written against it.**

`series-fashion-week-european-lens` closes 2026-10-06. It is the only dated
trigger in the fortnight and the library's second-largest folder exists to serve
it. Post A below is the answer, but it needed a calendar to have surfaced on its
own, and there isn't one.
