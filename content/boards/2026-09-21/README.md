# Outfit boards — 21 September 2026

Six runway looks, six outfits built from real product photographs, laid out as
flat-lay boards. One storyline: **I loved the colour combination in this look,
so I styled it.**

**All text below is publish-ready, and none of it is in your voice yet.**
`voice/` still holds only a README — no samples, no do/don't sheet — so nothing
here has been checked against how you actually write. Treat every Note as a
first draft of the words, not a final one. The CLAUDE.md no-published-text rule
was suspended for this session only, at your instruction.

**How to read this page.** Each board has an **A** version (the runway photograph
on the left) and a **B** version (a palette card instead, for when the photograph
can't be used). Both are 1080 × 1350. Two boards also have a 1456 px landscape
version for the post.

**What the rules are.** Every pairing named below is one of the five general
relationships: **opposition** (hues 100–180° apart, matched relative chroma,
ΔL\* ≥ 15), **tonal** (hues ≤ 40° apart, matched relative chroma, ΔL\* ≥ 25),
**muted** (hues 40–100° apart, both colours under 0.45 relative chroma, ΔL\* ≥ 25),
**monochrome** (two garments within ΔE 12 of each other), and **chromatic on a
neutral ground** (anything under 0.15 relative chroma is ground, and sits with
everything). Every outfit below was checked pair by pair; where a pair fails it
is said so.

> A pale blue coat over a warm brown shoe is the only thing I have looked at

## Board 2 — Tonal: one hue, three depths

<img src="board-2-a.jpg" width="360"> <img src="board-2-b.jpg" width="360">

**Note — pattern: *label then three-up* (learned from `swipe/formats/IMG_0074.jpeg`,
`IMG_0108.png`: a bare noun phrase, sometimes just two words and a colon, then
the images carry it).**

> One brown, three times over.
>
> Not a neutral outfit — a single hue held at three different depths, which is a
> different thing and much harder to get wrong. The rule is that the hues stay
> within about forty degrees of each other and the lightness steps stay wide. Do
> that and it reads as deliberate. Skip the lightness step and it reads as
> laundry.

**The pieces**

| # | Piece | Brand | Status in `brands/` | Hex |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Carlotta2* cotton corduroy double-breasted jacket, colour *Taupe* | Massimo Alba | **in** `brands/massimo-alba.yaml` | `#6A5540` |
| 2 | V-neck wool-cashmere knit | Aspesi | **in** `brands/aspesi.yaml` | `#B39D8B` |
| 3 | Cotton gabardine skirt, colour *Beige* | Aspesi | **in** `brands/aspesi.yaml` | `#BB9974` |
| 4 | Soft flat loafers with pull tab | soeur | not in `brands/` | `#232323` |
| 5 | Cashmere-and-silk bandana, deep brown | Massimo Alba | **in** `brands/massimo-alba.yaml` | `#281E1B` |

**The colour logic.** **2 against 1** is tonal — hue gap **3.5°**, Δ relative
chroma **0.16**, ΔL\* **28.5**. **2 against 3** is monochrome at **ΔE 6.6** — the
same colour twice, which the rules allow outright. Every other pair on the board
is also tonal: 1–3 (2.1°, ΔL\* 27.7), 1–5 (28.4°, ΔL\* 25.4), 3–5 (30.4°,
ΔL\* 53.1), 2–5 (24.9°, ΔL\* 53.8). It is the most internally consistent board
of the six.

**Image sources.** Inspiration: *someone else's photo / screenshot*
(`swipe/fashion shows ss27/IMG_0811.png`), house not legible, not named.
Pieces: *brand product shots*.

**Missing piece.** No bag. Nothing in the cut-out pool pairs with five browns
without going flat against one of them — what this needs is a bag around
**L\* 40–50**, mid-depth, so it lands between the jacket and the skirt.

## Board 4 — Muted: two quiet colours, far apart in lightness

<img src="board-4-a.jpg" width="360"> <img src="board-4-b.jpg" width="360">

> Faded pink and a dark olive.
>
> This is the quietest of the three ways two colours can sit together, and the
> one people talk about least. The hues are neither opposite nor neighbours —
> they are about seventy degrees apart, which is the awkward middle. It only

## Board 5 — One colour on a neutral ground

<img src="board-5-a.jpg" width="360"> <img src="board-5-b.jpg" width="360">

**Note — pattern: *verdict then three-up*, second use (`swipe/fashion shows ss27/IMG_0070.jpeg`).**

> A print coat with one red repeated three times — bag, hem, shoe.
>
> I do not own anything like that coat and I am not going to. What travels is
> the arithmetic underneath it: one saturated thing, everything else quiet
> enough to be furniture. Quiet has a number — under fifteen per cent of the
> saturation available at that lightness. Below that line a colour stops
> competing and becomes ground.

| 5 | Double-faced wool buttoned cape | soeur | not in `brands/` | `#6D6B6D` |

**The colour logic.** Piece 1 sits at **0.78** relative chroma. Pieces 2–5 sit at

**Image sources.** Inspiration: *someone else's photo / screenshot*

## Board 6 — Opposition at full strength

<img src="board-6-a.jpg" width="360"> <img src="board-6-b.jpg" width="360">

*Landscape version for the post:* <img src="board-6-a-wide.jpg" width="500">

**Note — pattern: *question* (learned from `swipe/formats/IMG_0095.png`: the
question is the first line, not the last, and the replies do the closing).**

> Would you put a mustard scarf on a pale blue shirt?
>
> I would, and here is why it is not a risk. Those two are almost exactly
> opposite on the wheel — a hundred and sixty-two degrees apart — and, more to
> the point, they are equally saturated. That second part is what people miss.
> Opposite hues at matched intensity vibrate pleasantly. Opposite hues at
> mismatched intensity just look like one of them is wrong.

**The pieces**

> family. The saturations match almost exactly. And the lightness gap is
> forty-two points, which is enormous. Close hue, matched intensity, wide

### Note C — "Denim is not a colour."

<img src="note-c-denim-is-not-a-colour.jpg" width="360">

**Pattern: *verdict* (`swipe/formats/IMG_0072.jpeg`).**

> Denim is not a colour, and any system that treats it as one will eventually
> tell you to get rid of your jeans.
>
> Two of the blues on this card are indistinguishable to a test drawn around
> "denim". One of them is denim. The other is a perfectly ordinary blue that a
> lot of people have in a knit. The only thing that separates them is what the

<img src="note-d-two-reds-one-step.jpg" width="360">
