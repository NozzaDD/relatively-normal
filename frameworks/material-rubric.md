# Material Rubric — does this brand or garment pass?

The source of truth for quality judgements. When this rubric has an answer, it
overrides general impressions about a brand, including good ones. Nothing is
described as well-made, durable or sustainable unless a field here supports it.

Five fields, each scored **0–3**. Maximum 15.

Score every field from evidence — a product page, a care label, a mill name, a
receipt. A field with no evidence is scored `unknown`, not 0: zero is a judgement
that the garment fails, unknown is an admission that nobody has looked yet.

---

## 1. Fibre content

What the garment is actually made of, from the composition label.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Majority synthetic with no performance reason — polyester or acrylic standing in for wool or cotton |
| 1 | Natural fibre majority, but blended down with synthetic, or a weak natural fibre (viscose, low-grade cotton) |
| 2 | Single natural fibre, ordinary grade — standard merino, good cotton, linen; or a blend where the synthetic earns its place (a technical shell, elastane under 5% in denim) |
| 3 | Single natural fibre, named grade — extra-fine merino, long-staple cotton, cashmere with a stated micron or ply, undyed or garment-dyed with the process named |

## 2. Construction markers

How it is put together. Read from photos, the product description and the garment
in hand.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Fused or glued where it should be stitched; raw overlocked seams inside; visible pattern break at the seams |
| 1 | Machine-made to a price — serged seams, thin lining, no reinforcement at stress points, no spare buttons or yarn |
| 2 | Considered finishing — matched patterns, reinforced stress points, full lining or a clean unlined finish, buttons sewn to last |
| 3 | Markers that cost real money — half or full canvas in tailoring, hand-set sleeves, loopwheeled or fully-fashioned knitwear, French or flat-felled seams, functional buttonholes |

## 3. Mill or origin

Where the cloth comes from and where the garment was made. Both matter; score the
weaker of the two when they differ.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Neither disclosed, or disclosed and evasive ("imported", "made in the EU" with no country) |
| 1 | Country of manufacture stated, cloth source not |
| 2 | Country of manufacture stated and credible for the category, cloth described by type and region |
| 3 | Mill named and verifiable, manufacture named — e.g. a Biella or Marche mill on the label, sewn in a country the brand names |

## 4. Price band

Not "is it expensive" — is the price coherent with fields 1–3. A cheap garment that
is honestly what it claims scores well here; an expensive one trading on a name
does not.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Price is a markup on a name — fields 1–3 are weak and the price is high anyway |
| 1 | Priced above what the fibre and construction justify, but not absurdly |
| 2 | Price matches the garment — you are paying for the cloth and the making |
| 3 | Priced below what the fibre and construction would predict — the reason H&M can appear here when it earns it |

## 5. Expected wear count

How many wears the garment plausibly survives in good condition, with normal care.
This is the field that decides price-per-wear, and it is a judgement, so record the
reasoning alongside the score.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | A season — pills, bags out, loses colour or shape within a few months |
| 1 | A year or two of regular wear before it looks tired; fine for a trend piece, wrong for a foundation |
| 2 | Five years of regular wear with ordinary care; holds shape and colour |
| 3 | A decade or more, and repairable — seams can be let out, knitwear can be rewoven, a cobbler or tailor can keep it going |

## Notes on use

- Score brands in `brands/` at the brand level as a typical-garment estimate, and
  individual garments separately where it matters. A brand-level score is a prior,
  not a verdict on a specific piece.
- Re-score when a brand changes ownership, moves production, or changes its
  composition labels. Record the date of the score.
- Field 3 is the field that most often turns out to be `unknown`. That is the
  honest answer until someone checks the label.
- A sustainability claim needs field 1 and field 5 to support it, and field 3 to be
  disclosed. Longevity plus known origin is the claim; a recycled-polyester label
  on a garment that lasts one season is not.
